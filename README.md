## About This Repository

This repository presents a reproducible demonstration case of structural analysis applied to chess.

Chess is used here not as a metaphor, but as a bounded system with explicit states, rules, actions, constraints, and verifiable outcomes. This makes it possible to demonstrate how structural analysis moves from the description of individual elements to the analysis of relationships, available actions, dependencies, and conditions required for a result.

The case develops from a simple observation:

> A system may retain all of its resources while losing the ability to perform a required function.

The analysis begins with concrete chess positions, introduces controlled changes to their conditions, tests alternative explanations, and concludes with a bounded mathematical formalization.

## What the Case Contains

The complete manual consists of seven analytical blocks:

1. **Problem Definition and Reproducible Demonstration**
2. **Structural Model of a Chess Position**
3. **Functions and Dependencies of Elements**
4. **A Move as a Transformation of the System**
5. **Testing by Varying Conditions**
6. **Mathematical Formalization**
7. **Final Review and Assembly**

Together, these blocks examine:

- system states;
- available and unavailable actions;
- functional dependencies;
- constraints;
- alternative acting elements;
- sequencing effects;
- controlled changes of conditions;
- counterexamples;
- bounded outcome verification;
- mathematical representation of states, actions, transitions, and task conditions.

## Why Chess?

Chess provides a useful demonstration environment because its rules are explicit and its states can be reproduced.

This allows structural claims to be tested rather than merely described.

The purpose of the case is not to propose a new chess strategy. It is to demonstrate how a structural analysis can identify which conditions make a particular action or outcome possible, unavailable, or dependent on other elements of the system.

## Mathematical Formalization

The case develops a bounded mathematical specification connecting:

**state → relations → available actions → transitions → task conditions → outcome**

The formal model is deliberately limited to the mechanisms examined in the demonstration case. It is not presented as a complete mathematical model of chess.

## Relevance Beyond Chess

The broader research direction concerns the structural analysis and mathematical modelling of complex systems.

In organizational and technical environments, the same analytical questions can be asked:

- What state is the system actually in?
- Which actions are available?
- Which functions depend on other elements?
- Which constraints prevent an otherwise possible action?
- Which conditions must remain available for a task to be completed?
- What changes when the order of operations changes?
- Which conclusions remain valid when conditions are modified?

Transfer to another domain requires its own model, data, assumptions, and validation. The chess case does not by itself establish results for business, industrial, or AI systems.

## AI and Mathematical Modelling

A related research direction at Nelvarx Systems AB investigates how formal structural models can support the adaptation, training, and evaluation of AI systems for specific organizational and technical environments.

The objective is not simply to provide AI with more documents, but to define the states, relationships, constraints, actions, and outcome criteria that the system must distinguish.

This direction is under development and is not claimed as a result demonstrated by the present chess case.
